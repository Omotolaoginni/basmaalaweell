# 1. Product Overview

## 1.1 Product Summary

The Executive Portfolio is a premium, editorial-style personal-brand and professional engagement website for an experienced executive working across inclusive economic growth, community development, philanthropy, nonprofit leadership, and cross-sector collaboration.

The website will communicate the client's professional identity, leadership experience, areas of expertise, selected work, speaking engagements, published writing, and opportunities for professional engagement.

It will serve as a central digital destination where prospective partners, philanthropic organisations, nonprofit leaders, government representatives, private-sector executives, event organisers, and other relevant stakeholders can understand the client's work and initiate meaningful professional conversations.

The website must communicate credibility, leadership, experience, and measurable impact without becoming a conventional online CV, generic corporate website, product designer portfolio, or agency platform.

## 1.2 Product Vision

To establish a credible and distinctive digital presence that communicates the client's leadership, experience, perspective, and contribution to inclusive social and economic development while creating clear pathways for meaningful professional engagement.

## 1.3 Brand Headline

**Big Ideas, Real Impact.**

This headline should communicate the relationship between strategic thinking, collaborative leadership, and measurable outcomes.

## 1.4 Product Positioning

The website is an executive personal-brand and professional engagement platform.

It should position the client as a strategic leader and collaborator capable of connecting people, institutions, resources, and ideas to advance inclusive economic growth and community-driven change.

The website should reflect the client's combination of:

- More than 15 years of professional experience.
- Experience across engineering, finance, and nonprofit leadership.
- Leadership informed by professional expertise and lived refugee experience.
- Cross-sector collaboration involving nonprofits, government, philanthropy, and the private sector.
- Programme development, organisational growth, and strategic execution.
- Commitment to equity, representation, collaboration, and measurable impact.

All biographical and professional claims must be verified against client-approved information before publication.

# 3. Problem Statement

The client requires a central digital platform that communicates her professional identity, leadership experience, expertise, and impact to a diverse audience of stakeholders.

Without a well-structured executive website:

- Visitors may struggle to understand the breadth and relevance of her experience.
- Professional achievements may appear disconnected across different sectors and roles.
- Potential partners may not understand the types of engagements she can support.
- Speaking organisers may have difficulty identifying her areas of expertise.
- Published writing and thought leadership may not have a clear central destination.
- Interested organisations may lack an obvious pathway to initiate contact.

The Executive Portfolio must address these challenges through clear information architecture, credible storytelling, deliberate content hierarchy, and functional engagement pathways.

# 7. Visitor Journey

The website should support the following visitor journey:

**Discover → Understand → Evaluate → Engage → Connect**

## 7.1 Discover

Visitors arrive through search engines, professional networks, referrals, speaking engagements, publications, or direct links.

The homepage should establish the client's identity, positioning, and value proposition.

## 7.2 Understand

Visitors explore the About Me section to understand her background, leadership experience, professional journey, and philosophy.

## 7.3 Evaluate

Visitors review the Work With Me, Speaking, and Writing sections to assess her expertise, relevance, and potential value to their organisation.

## 7.4 Engage

Visitors identify a relevant opportunity, review available information, and select an appropriate call to action.

## 7.5 Connect

Visitors initiate contact through the approved contact channel.

The website must make this journey clear without forcing visitors through a rigid sequence.

# 9. Information Architecture

## 9.1 Primary Navigation

The website must provide access to:

| Section | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Home | Introduce the client, positioning, and primary engagement opportunities. |
| About Me | Present professional background, leadership journey, experience, and philosophy. |
| Work With Me | Explain areas of expertise and potential professional engagements. |
| Speaking | Present speaking themes, relevant engagements, and inquiry opportunities. |
| Writing | Showcase published articles, interviews, essays, and thought leadership. |
| Contact Me | Provide clear channels for professional communication. |

## 9.2 Navigation Architecture Decision

The six sections are mandatory navigation destinations.

Whether they are implemented as separate pages or as anchor-based sections within a single page must be confirmed during repository and architecture inspection.

The implementation must not create inconsistent navigation behaviour or inaccessible content as a result of this decision.

If separate pages are used:

- Each page must have a distinct, stable URL.
- Direct navigation to each URL must work.
- Browser refresh must not produce an application error.
- Internal links must resolve correctly.
- Page titles and metadata must be appropriate to each page.

If a single-page structure is used:

- Each major section must have a stable, meaningful anchor.
- Navigation must move visitors to the correct section.
- Browser history and direct section links should behave predictably.
- Sticky navigation must not obscure section headings.
- Active navigation states must reflect the current section where appropriate.

The final route and URL convention must be documented in `architecture.md` and validated during implementation.

## 9.3 Global Navigation Requirements

The global navigation must:

- Provide access to all six primary destinations.
- Maintain visual consistency throughout the website.
- Clearly communicate the current page or active section.
- Support keyboard navigation.
- Provide visible focus states.
- Work on desktop, tablet, and mobile.
- Prevent menu content from being clipped by the viewport.
- Include an accessible mobile navigation pattern.
- Avoid unnecessary navigation complexity.

The final navigation layout and interaction pattern should follow the approved design.

The final contact method must be confirmed before implementation.

Possible approaches:

1. Direct email link.
2. Contact form connected to an approved delivery service.
3. A combination of both.

If a contact form is implemented, it must:

- Provide clearly labelled fields.
- Validate required information.
- Validate email address format.
- Display field-specific error messages.
- Prevent accidental duplicate submissions.
- Display a loading state during submission.
- Provide a clear success state after confirmed delivery or successful acceptance by the configured service.
- Display a useful error state if submission fails.
- Preserve entered information when a recoverable submission error occurs.
- Provide an appropriate spam prevention mechanism.
- Explain how submitted information will be used.

- Name.
- Email address.

1. Visitor enters information.
2. Client-side validation checks field requirements.
3. Submission is sent through the approved endpoint or service.
4. The receiving system performs server-side validation.
5. The service processes the message.
6. The website receives a response.
7. The website displays the appropriate success or error state.

The form delivery provider, backend endpoint, email routing, and notification destination are **TBD**.

The implementation must not expose private API keys, credentials, or service secrets in browser code.

- Email links open the intended address.
- Form submissions reach the approved destination if a form is implemented.
- Invalid submissions are rejected with clear feedback.
- Loading, success, and failure states work.
- The visitor is informed about the handling of submitted information.
- No sensitive credentials are exposed.

# 11. Detailed Interaction Requirements

All interactive elements must provide clear, predictable, and accessible feedback.

## 11.1 Navigation

- Navigation links must lead to their intended destinations.
- The current page or active section must be distinguishable.
- Mobile navigation must open and close reliably.
- The mobile menu must support keyboard interaction.
- The menu must not trap keyboard focus incorrectly.
- Escape must close an open mobile menu or dismissible navigation panel.
- Selecting a navigation destination must close the mobile menu where appropriate.
- Focus must remain visible throughout navigation.
- If an overlay is used, its interaction with the underlying page must be managed appropriately.

## 11.2 Buttons and Links

Interactive elements must include appropriate states:

- Default.
- Hover, where a pointer is available.
- Focus-visible.
- Active or pressed.
- Disabled, where applicable.

Requirements:

- Buttons must perform an action.
- Links must navigate to a meaningful destination.
- External links must be distinguishable where necessary.
- Decorative elements must not appear interactive.
- Buttons and links must not use placeholder destinations.
- The visual distinction between primary and secondary actions must remain consistent.

## 11.3 Interactive Content

Any expandable or dismissible interface must:

- Communicate its current state.
- Support keyboard operation.
- Use appropriate accessible attributes.
- Provide a clear close or dismissal mechanism.
- Avoid hiding important information from assistive technology.

The implementation should not introduce complex interactions when a simple link or static content presentation is sufficient.

## 11.4 Motion and Animation

- Motion must support usability rather than distract from content.
- Essential content must not depend on animation to become visible.
- Motion must not prevent access to navigation or information.
- Reduced-motion preferences must be respected.
- Avoid unnecessary parallax, scroll hijacking, continuous motion, and decorative animation.
- Animation must not introduce layout shifts or interfere with performance.

# 14. Accessibility Requirements

## 14.1 Accessibility Standard

Target **WCAG 2.2 Level AA**.

Accessibility must be considered throughout design and implementation rather than treated as a final visual check.

## 14.2 Semantic Structure

- Use appropriate HTML landmarks.
- Use one clear primary page heading.
- Maintain logical heading order.
- Use semantic buttons for actions.
- Use links for navigation.
- Use labels for form controls.
- Use appropriate list and article semantics where relevant.
- Avoid using generic elements in place of meaningful HTML elements.

## 14.3 Keyboard Accessibility

- All essential interactions must be keyboard accessible.
- Focus order must be logical.
- Focus indicators must be visible.
- No essential information may be available only through hover.
- Navigation and menus must support keyboard operation.
- Dismissible elements must have a predictable keyboard interaction.

## 14.4 Colour and Contrast

Minimum contrast requirements:

- Normal text: 4.5:1.
- Large text: 3:1.
- Essential interface components and graphical indicators: 3:1 where WCAG requires it.

Colour must not be the only method used to communicate information.

Contrast must be tested using actual foreground and background combinations.

## 14.5 Images and Media

- Meaningful images must have descriptive alternative text.
- Decorative images must use appropriate empty alternative text.
- Videos must provide captions where speech or meaningful audio is present.
- Media controls must be accessible.
- Image content must not be the only source of essential information.

## 14.6 Forms

- Every input must have an associated label.
- Required fields must be identified.
- Errors must be described in text.
- Error messages must be associated with the relevant fields.
- Submission feedback must be announced appropriately to assistive technology.
- Keyboard users must be able to complete and submit the form.

## 14.7 Accessibility Testing

Before launch, test:

- Keyboard-only navigation.
- Visible focus states.
- Screen-reader navigation.
- Heading structure.
- Colour contrast.
- Form labels and validation.
- Alternative text.
- Browser zoom.
- Reduced-motion preferences.
- Mobile navigation accessibility.

Automated accessibility tools may support the process but do not replace manual testing.

# 15. Performance Requirements

## 15.1 Performance Objective

The website must load efficiently and remain responsive across typical mobile and desktop connections.

Performance is especially important because visitors may access the website through mobile devices and varying network conditions.

## 15.2 Core Web Vitals Targets

The following are proposed performance targets based on Google's recommended good thresholds:

| Metric | Target |
|---|---:|
| Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) | 2.5 seconds or less |
| Interaction to Next Paint (INP) | 200 milliseconds or less |
| Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) | 0.1 or less |

Where sufficient real-user data is available, these targets should be evaluated at the 75th percentile.

Before real-user data exists, use appropriate laboratory testing and report the limitations of the results.

These metrics are targets, not guaranteed results.

## 15.3 Image Optimisation

- Use appropriately sized images.
- Prefer modern image formats where supported.
- Avoid unnecessarily large image downloads.
- Use responsive image techniques where appropriate.
- Define image dimensions or aspect ratios to reduce layout shifts.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold images.
- Do not lazy-load the primary above-the-fold image when doing so would delay the main content.
- Use approved compression without visibly degrading image quality.

## 15.4 Font Optimisation

- Load only required font families and weights.
- Avoid unnecessary font files.
- Use appropriate font-display behaviour.
- Prevent major layout shifts during font loading.
- Confirm that Cormorant Garamond and DM Sans load correctly.
- Provide sensible fallback fonts.

## 15.5 JavaScript and Rendering

- Avoid unnecessary dependencies.
- Avoid shipping code that is not required by the MVP.
- Minimise render-blocking resources.
- Avoid unnecessary client-side processing.
- Use the rendering approach supported by the existing project architecture.
- Prevent avoidable layout shifts and expensive visual effects.

## 15.6 Performance Acceptance Criteria

- No unnecessary large assets are shipped.
- Images are appropriately optimised.
- Fonts load without major visual instability.
- No significant performance regression is introduced by new components.
- The agreed performance targets are tested and documented before launch.

The Executive Portfolio is a responsive, accessible, editorial-style executive personal-brand and professional engagement website.

1. Home
2. About Me
3. Work With Me
4. Speaking
5. Writing
6. Contact Me
